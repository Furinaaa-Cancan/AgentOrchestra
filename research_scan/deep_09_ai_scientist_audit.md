# Deep-Dive 09: AI Scientist / Autonomous Research — Novelty Audit Opportunity

## 1. State of the field (2024–2026)

**Sakana AI Scientist v1/v2.** Sakana's v2 (Apr 2025) drops human-coded templates in favor of agentic tree search across idea generation, code, figures, manuscript and review. Sakana claims v2 produced the first fully AI-generated paper to pass workshop peer review, and a Nature paper followed (Sakana announcements). The system is open-source on GitHub.

**OpenAI MLE-Bench / PaperBench.** MLE-Bench (75 Kaggle competitions) had o1-preview + AIDE reach Kaggle-bronze on 16.9% of tasks at launch; by Sep 2025 a follow-up pushed MLE-Bench-lite medal rate from 39.6% to 47.7%. PaperBench (Apr 2025) asks agents to replicate 20 ICML 2024 Spotlight/Oral papers from scratch; best agent (Claude 3.5 Sonnet + scaffold) scored 21.0%, vs ML-PhD humans at 41.4% on a 3-paper subset.

**Benchmarks for "research" itself.** ResearchBench (Mar 2025) decomposes scientific discovery into inspiration retrieval, hypothesis composition and ranking across 12 disciplines, restricted to post-2024 papers to mitigate contamination. LMR-Bench, MLRC-Bench, InnovatorBench all target ML-research reproduction or method invention.

**Domain systems.**
- *Materials*: DeepMind GNoME (Nature 2023) — 380k "stable" candidate crystals.
- *Chemistry*: ChemCrow (Nature Machine Intelligence 2024) — GPT-4 + 18 tools, planned syntheses of an insect repellent and organocatalysts. ORGANA, A-Lab, and a growing pile of "self-driving labs" reviewed in Royal Society Open Science (Jul 2025) and Chem Reviews (2024).
- *Biology*: SAMPLE (Nature Chem Eng 2024) and a generalized autonomous-enzyme-engineering platform (Nat Comm 2025).

## 2. Skeptical evaluations and reproducibility audits

The critical literature is now substantial enough to be its own thing:

- **Beel et al., "Evaluating Sakana's AI Scientist" (arXiv 2502.14297, SIGIR Forum 2025).** Headline findings: 42% of generated experiments fail on coding errors; "novelty" judgements misclassify well-known concepts (e.g. micro-batching SGD) as novel; literature review is keyword-search, not synthesis; the user-supplied template carries most of the autonomy load.
- **Cheetham & Seshadri (UCSB, Apr 2024)** on GNoME — "scant evidence for compounds that fulfil the trifecta of novelty, credibility, and utility"; most "materials" are crystalline inorganics relabeled.
- **A-Lab critique (Feb 2024)**: of 35 "novel" auto-synthesized compounds, most were mixtures of known phases and three were not new at all.
- **C&EN (Dec 2025)**: >10% of GNoME's "stable" crystals are near-duplicates of structures already in databases.
- **ChemCrow reproducibility**: relies on closed GPT-4 API; LLM-as-judge biases evaluations toward GPT-4 outputs; non-deterministic.
- **Contamination literature**: Auditing Test Data Contamination with FDR control (OpenReview 2025), CoDeC ICL-based detection, watermarking benchmarks (ICLR 2025), DCR (EMNLP 2025), and "Quantifying the Effect of Test Set Contamination on Generative Evaluations" (2026) — show memorization-vs-generalization is empirically separable but rarely separated in "AI discovers X" papers.

## 3. The hard question: how do you verify a "discovery" is not retrieval?

There is no agreed methodology. Practical signals used piecemeal:
1. **Temporal hold-out**: restrict the claim to entities/papers post-dating the pretraining cutoff (ResearchBench does this).
2. **Database deduplication**: structural/semantic nearest-neighbor search against existing corpora (this is what caught GNoME's near-duplicates).
3. **Wet-lab or independent re-execution**: the A-Lab attempt — exposed the brittleness rather than validating it.
4. **Counterfactual prompting**: ask the system to "discover" something that was deliberately excluded from training; check it doesn't.
5. **Human expert blind review**: still the gold standard, but expensive and itself biased toward AI's fluent prose (see ChemCrow LLM-judge bias).

No single audit framework combines (1)-(5). This is the gap.

## 4. Paper opportunities

### Proposal A — "Memorization-vs-Discovery: A Contamination-Controlled Audit of AI-Scientist Outputs in ML"

*Scope feasible for a non-domain-expert ML researcher.* Take 100–300 ideas/hypotheses generated by Sakana AI Scientist v2 and 2–3 open MLE/Research benchmarks. For each:
- Embed the idea, retrieve top-k nearest neighbors from arXiv, OpenReview, and the model's known pretraining slice (e.g. The Pile, Dolma); compute textual + semantic novelty scores.
- Run an MIA / membership-inference probe (Min-K%, DC-PDD) on the *seed prompt* and on the *generated hypothesis*.
- Have 3 domain reviewers blind-rate novelty; compare to automated scores.
- Headline metric: fraction of "novel" claims whose nearest neighbor is within ε of pretraining text.

Deliverable: an open audit tool ("NoveltyAudit") + a calibration paper. Low domain-expertise barrier: all artifacts are text, infrastructure is standard LLM eval.

**Feasibility: high.** No wet-lab, no chem/bio expertise required. Hardest part is curating the reference corpus and getting reviewer time. 2–4 months solo.

### Proposal B — "ResearchLeak: A Held-Out Benchmark for True Discovery"

Construct a benchmark of ~200 *retracted-then-restated*, *post-cutoff*, and *deliberately-excluded* scientific claims with verified ground truth. Models are asked to "discover" the claim from background context. Scoring decomposes into: (i) retrieval from training data (penalty), (ii) recombination of training data (partial credit), (iii) genuinely held-out inference (full credit). Pair with a watermarking step (cf. ICLR 2025) so future contamination is detectable.

Differentiates from ResearchBench by *requiring* a memorization-vs-inference decomposition rather than just freshness.

**Feasibility: moderate.** Curation is the hard cost. Could collaborate with a domain group for ground truth; the ML/benchmark engineering is squarely in scope.

## 5. Honest assessment for a non-domain-expert researcher

What is *not* feasible solo: doing the GNoME-style materials reanalysis, judging chemistry synthesis claims, or running a wet-lab replication. Those require specific expertise and equipment, and the existing critiques (Cheetham/Seshadri, A-Lab, C&EN) are already strong.

What *is* feasible and underserved: the **meta-methodological audit** — treating "AI scientist" outputs as a *generation task* and applying the contamination/MIA toolkit that the LLM-eval community has built since 2024. Almost nobody has done this rigorously to Sakana v2 outputs, and Sakana's own novelty check is the system's weakest link by its critics' own account.

Recommended posture: Proposal A as the primary bet, with Proposal B as the longer follow-up. Both lean on tooling you already build for your multi-agent SWE-bench work (LLM eval harnesses, log analysis), which is a meaningful overlap-of-skills argument.

## 6. Deep-interaction challenge

The strongest *XY* risk here: you may be drawn to this because "AI Scientist" is hot, not because it advances your underlying agenda of *multi-agent reliability and failure modes*. Proposal A is defensible only if you frame it as **"novelty hallucination as a failure mode of agentic LLMs"** — i.e. an extension of your existing MAST-style diagnostic work, not a pivot into philosophy-of-science. If you cannot honestly tie it back to your v3 agenda in one sentence, drop it and stick with SWE-bench diagnostics.

A cheaper alternative path: publish **NoveltyAudit as a tool/short paper** (NeurIPS D&B or an arXiv preprint) rather than a full empirical study. Maximum signal, minimum scope creep.
